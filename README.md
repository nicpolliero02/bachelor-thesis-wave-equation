# Linear and semilinear wave equation: a first overview
Bachelor's thesis in Mathematics at the University of Padua, exploring linear and nonlinear wave equations, with a focus on existence, uniqueness, and regularity of solutions.
The first chapter introduces the linear wave equation and derives classical solution formulas, including d'Alembert's formula, Kirchhoff's formula, and the method of dimensional reduction, as well as the treatment of nonhomogeneous equations through Duhamel's principle.
The second chapter develops the functional-analytic framework required to study nonlinear partial differential equations. 
It covers weak derivatives, Sobolev spaces and their completeness and density properties, Fourier transforms and Sobolev regularity, and Bochner spaces for time-dependent functions taking values in Banach spaces. 
Difference quotients are introduced as a tool for analysing the regularity of weak solutions.
The third chapter applies these tools to nonlinear wave equations.
For the quasilinear problem, it develops existence and uniqueness results using the Banach fixed-point theorem and continuous dependence on initial data, with energy estimates and difference quotients playing a central role in the analysis of solution regularity. 
For the semilinear problem, it uses energy methods and functional-analytic techniques, including compactness arguments and the Aubin–Lions lemma and Hahn–Banach theorem, to establish existence results under suitable assumptions. 
The final part focuses on the three-dimensional semilinear wave equation, studying the construction of smooth solutions and the characterization of their maximal existence time.
